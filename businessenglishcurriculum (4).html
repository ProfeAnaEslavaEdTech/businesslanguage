Business English

A CEFR-anchored curriculum built with the MLLDE method — the four-layer model, CLIL 4Cs, and the IPO lesson cycle — mapped scenario-by-scenario from A2 to C1. Language-agnostic method, filled with Business English data.

CEFR A2 → C1CLIL 4CsIPO lesson cycle12 unitsMediation @ B2+No AI-voice component
Golden rule applied. The method is language-agnostic: the levels and can-do targets come from CEFR (universal); only the filling — vocabulary, functions, grammar, culture — is Business English. The mandatory "Don't give up!" step is delivered as a rubric + teacher/self-check — no AI mentor.

4Flagship unit · B1 "Kicking Off the Project" ★

Scenario: chairing a project kick-off with a cross-functional team · CEFR B1. Modes: Reception (model dialogue) · Production (follow-up email / spoken summary) · Interaction (the chairing mission). Mediation: not yet (enters at B2).

ContentProject basics: agenda, milestone, deliverable, stakeholder, action item, deadline.
CommunicationChairing language — opening, handing over, moving on, assigning, summarising.
CognitionSequencing an agenda; prioritising action items by owner and deadline.
CultureMeeting etiquette in international teams; diplomatic vs. direct register; turn-taking.
CompetenceBusiness English items realised
Pragmatic
(functions)		Opening a meeting · managing the agenda (moving on) · assigning tasks · summarising · agreeing next steps
Linguistic
(grammar/lexis)		Present simple for agenda/facts; will for offers/decisions; polite question forms; project lexis (agenda, milestone, deliverable, stakeholder, action item, deadline)
SociolinguisticRegister: "Could you…?" / "Would you mind…?" vs. bare imperatives; punctuality & turn-taking in international teams
  • Vocabulary: agenda · milestone · deliverable · stakeholder · action item · deadline
  • Chunks: "Let's get started, shall we?" · "Let's move on to…" · "Could you take care of…?" · "I'd like to hand over to…" · "To sum up, …" · "Let's schedule a follow-up for…"
  • Mission (authentic assessment): chair a 3-turn kick-off — state the objective, assign ≥2 action items with named owners + deadlines, and agree the next meeting.
  • "Don't give up!" (Immediate Assessment): learner writes/says a chairing turn; checked against target chunks + a minimum-length gate; feedback in amber (never red), specific with an example, unlimited retry. Success → coins + "Project Launch" badge. Rubric-based; no AI mentor.
  • Culture & pragmatics: diplomatic > blunt; start/end on time; hand over clearly on calls.

5Unit specs (anchor targets)

Each follows the same template; anchor targets, functions and mediation notes to fill on build.

  • A2 · Introductions & small talk — Interaction. Greeting, introducing self/others, asking about role/company, safe topics. Formal vs. first-name cultures.
  • A2 · Simple emails — Production (written). Request, reply, thank; email openings/closings, please/could.
  • B1 · Telephoning & scheduling — Interaction. Identifying self, connecting, arranging/changing times: "Could I speak to…?", "Would Thursday suit you?"
  • B1 · Describing company/role/product — Production. Responsibilities, company facts, product features; present simple, comparatives.
  • B2 · Meetings & decision-making — Interaction. Giving/supporting opinion, agreeing/disagreeing diplomatically, interrupting politely; hedging.
  • B2 · Negotiating — Interaction. Proposing, conditional bargaining (If you…, we could…), rejecting softly, compromising; conditionals.
  • B2 · Presentations — Production. Signposting, describing trends/data, handling Q&A; intro–body–close.
  • B2 · Handling complaints — Interaction + Mediation (entry). Apologising, clarifying, proposing solutions; mediating client ↔ internal team.
  • C1 · Strategic negotiation — Interaction. Framing, managing objections, trading concessions, closing; tone control.
  • C1 · Persuasive pitches — Production. Persuasion, rhetorical structure, anticipating challenge; call to action.
  • C1 · Reporting & briefing — Mediation (flagship). Summarise a long report, simplify for a non-expert, brief across teams/cultures.

6Assessment & feedback

Per MLLDE §8 — minus the AI mentor.

  • Authentic assessment = the gate. Mastery of the real-world Mission, not a test score.
  • Feedback ladder → human. Self-check against a rubric → peer/teacher feedback → optional recorded model answer.
  • "Don't give up!" kept. Errors in amber with 🤔, never red; specific + example; retry always offered; success celebrated (coins + badge).

7Build order · SAM

Produce with the Successive Approximation Model: prototype → review → refine.

  • 1. B1 Project Kick-off (flagship, fully specified) — build first as the reference instance.
  • 2. B2 Negotiating + Presentations (highest workplace demand).
  • 3. A2 Introductions/Emails (on-ramp).
  • 4. C1 units + the C1 Mediation unit (top of the ladder).
