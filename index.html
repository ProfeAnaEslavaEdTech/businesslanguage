Curriculum Design · MLLDE-aligned

Business English

A CEFR-anchored curriculum built with the MLLDE method — the four-layer model, CLIL 4Cs, and the IPO lesson cycle — mapped scenario-by-scenario from A1 to C2. Language-agnostic method, filled with Business English data.

CEFR A1 → C2CLIL 4CsIPO lesson cycle72 units36 lessons per levelMediation @ A2+≥2 samples to computeNo AI-voice component
Viewing as
This is a face-to-face lesson plan. Nothing is scored automatically and nothing is measured while you teach. The Talketika minimum is agreed before class so that what happens in class, what is validated afterwards and the homework all describe the same thing. The teacher guides the process — every check below is guidance, never a gate.

1The 36 lessons of this level

Twelve units, three lessons each, delivered as three blocks of 12 classes — one block per validation cycle. The three passes are A · input & practice, B · processing & rehearsal, C · mission & validation, and their homework is spaced +1 / +3 / +7 days: the SRS ladder and the lesson sequence are the same thing. Three exposures clear the ≥2-sample threshold with one to spare. Click a lesson to open its plan, pre-filled from the curriculum. Status is saved in this browser only.

Pick the level. The board opens on B1; the other five are one click away, each with its own twelve units and thirty-six lessons.

2Course sources

The same import model the lesson plan uses, one level up: what this course is built on. Add the materials the group should meet across the 36 lessons — a site, a channel, a report, a set of documents — and the Goal Plan can be refreshed against them without moving a single CEFR target.


      
Sources never move the targets. The levels, the can-do statements and the mission of each unit come from CEFR and stay fixed. A source can change the scenario, the examples and the material — never the objective. Anything a source cannot cover comes back as a declared gap.

    


    

      
3Skill coverage across the course

      
The four CEFR skills — reading, listening, speaking, writing — plus mediation. Each needs at least two lessons that work it; one exposure says nothing about a learner. Grammar and vocabulary are not here: they are resources that feed production, and every unit carries its own. Amber is a warning, not a block.

      

      
Coverage counts the lessons you have planned, not attendance, and it counts a skill when the unit works it or the homework targets it. It is a planning check — nothing here measures a learner.

    


    

      
4The Talketika minimum · before class

      
Five things agreed before the class starts. They are what make the lesson, the validation and the homework describe the same thing.

      

        
#MinimumWhy it exists

Everything else — gamification, culture cards, the exact timing — is the teacher’s call.

    

  

  
Golden rule applied. The method is language-agnostic: the levels and can-do targets come from CEFR (universal); only the filling — vocabulary, functions, grammar, culture — is Business English. The mandatory "Don't give up!" step is delivered as a rubric + teacher/self-check — no AI mentor.


  
  

    
1The alignment

    
MLLDE plugs a universal standard into a per-language content reference. For English, the analog to Spanish's Plan curricular del Instituto Cervantes is English Profile + Cambridge Business English.

    

      
MLLDE layerBusiness English anchorSource

Domain reference — business can-doCambridge Business English — BEC Preliminary (B1) · Vantage (B2) · Higher (C1); Linguaskill BusinessCambridge Assessment English

        
Method — target → lessonMLLDE IPO cycle + CLIL 4Cs + SAM buildMLLDE Master Document

        
ProductThe 72 Business English units below — 6 levels × 12 units × 3 lessons—

      
    

    
Every unit is specified against the CEFR four modes (Reception · Production · Interaction · Mediation) and three competences (Linguistic · Sociolinguistic · Pragmatic). Mediation enters at A2+ — see the note below.

    
Why mediation starts at A2 here, not B2. The B2 gate is the app’s: mediation is hard to instrument automatically, so the product defers it. A classroom has no such constraint. An A2 learner mediates every working day — passing on a phone message without losing a detail, explaining a price, telling a colleague what the notice says — and a teacher in the room can judge whether the message survived. Deferring that to B2 would describe the tooling, not the learner. The CEFR Companion Volume puts relaying descriptors well below B2; face to face we follow the standard, and the app keeps its own gate for its own reasons.

4Flagship unit · B1 "Kicking Off the Project" ★

    
Scenario: chairing a project kick-off with a cross-functional team · CEFR B1. Modes: Reception (model dialogue) · Production (follow-up email / spoken summary) · Interaction (the chairing mission). Mediation: not yet (enters at B2).


    

      
ContentProject basics: agenda, milestone, deliverable, stakeholder, action item, deadline.

      
CommunicationChairing language — opening, handing over, moving on, assigning, summarising.

      
CognitionSequencing an agenda; prioritising action items by owner and deadline.

      
CultureMeeting etiquette in international teams; diplomatic vs. direct register; turn-taking.

Every unit is specified minimally on the same template: scenario · can-do target · mode focus · core vocabulary · chunks · grammar · mission · rubric · culture note · homework. That is the anchor; the teacher completes it before class.

    
Class hours are not the whole story. 36 lessons × 50 minutes ≈ 30 contact hours per level. Cambridge associates roughly 90–200 guided hours with a level jump, so homework and real use at work have to carry the rest: finishing the 36 lessons is a programme completed, not a level gained. Say that to a client before they infer it. (The app’s 80-effective-hours rule is a different thing — it measures screen activity, which a classroom does not have. Face to face, the claim rests on taught hours and on what the teacher has actually seen the learner produce.)

GYour source · develop the materials

      
The Goal Plan above fixes the destination; the source supplies the material. The source is checked against the plan, never the other way round — if it does not give occasion to practise a target chunk, that is a declared gap, not a lowered target.

      
What belongs here is what the discipline actually runs on. Business English is taught on its own genres, so the material worth attaching is a real one: an email thread, a set of minutes, a project one-pager or a slide, a report extract, a supplier quote, a recorded call, a customer complaint, a press piece about the sector. A general-interest article can carry the vocabulary, but only a work genre carries the register — and register is most of what a B1 learner is short of.

In the Teacher Dashboard this must be enforced server-side — a teacher’s token must not be able to fetch the curriculum payload or this spec at all, whatever the interface shows. Treat the role switcher here as a description of the requirement, not as its implementation.
